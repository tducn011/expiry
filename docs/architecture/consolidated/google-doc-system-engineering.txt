Cross-Domain Household Expiry and Lifecycle Management: Evidence, Personas, and Business Requirements
Executive summary
Published primary-user research does exist beyond food, but the evidence is uneven across domains. Food has the strongest combination of behavioral interviews and real-world app deployments: qualitative studies with household food managers, the TotalCtrl app evaluation, the 52-household CozZo field intervention, and the EATiT! deployment all converge on visibility loss, planning difficulty, maintenance burden, and physical-versus-digital inventory drift. [1] Cosmetics now has unusually relevant direct evidence: a 2025 peer-reviewed study used 33 semi-structured interviews in the UK and China specifically to investigate why consumers continue using cosmetics beyond expiry guidance. [2] Medicine has strong household evidence: an Australian study conducted semi-structured interviews with 166 experienced medicine users/households and identified 2,301 medicines, while newer Finnish and 2026 mixed-method work further documents unnecessary and expired medicines retained in homes. [3] Warranty and generic consumable-replacement research is materially weaker: strong repair-behavior research exists, including a Norwegian study with 1,196 consumers plus 15 repair-industry interviews, but I did not find comparably strong primary research specifically showing consumers need a generic “warranty expiry tracker.” [4] The evidence therefore does not justify one generic model such as product → expiry date → warning → expired. It does justify testing a broader household lifecycle-management architecture whose shared layer tracks ownership, lifecycle events, attention and actions, while food, cosmetics, medicines and warranty remain separate domain policies. For an MVP, the evidence supports food first, with cosmetics as the strongest second vertical; medicine should come later behind safety/content governance, and warranty should remain exploratory until direct user validation.
Research questions and evidence standard
The research problem should not be framed as “Do users want an expiry app?” That already assumes both the problem and the solution. The governing question is:
Do users across multiple household-product domains experience sufficiently similar lifecycle-management failures that one shared product can solve them without erasing domain-specific meaning, safety rules, and actions?
The deep-research questions should therefore be:
Research question
	Why it matters
	RQ — Prevalence: How often do households actually retain food, cosmetics or medicines beyond relevant lifecycle dates, or miss warranty/replacement events?
	Determines whether this is a meaningful recurring problem rather than an attractive app concept.
	RQ — Measurement: Is prevalence measured through household audits, observed inventory, diary records, or retrospective self-report?
	Interview recall can reveal why behavior occurs but is weak for estimating actual quantities/frequency.
	RQ — Lifecycle: What event actually starts or changes the lifecycle clock: manufacture, purchase, opening, storage change, dispensing, first use, product failure, or elapsed usage?
	A single expiryDate field cannot represent all domains correctly.
	RQ — Visibility: At what stage does the product stop being actively remembered or understood by its owner?
	Tests whether “forgetting expiry” is actually an attention/inventory-visibility problem.
	RQ — Capture: Where can lifecycle data come from: printed date, PAO symbol, barcode, receipt, email invoice, manufacturer data, photo/OCR, or user entry?
	Determines onboarding and recurring-maintenance burden.
	RQ — State drift: What happens after users consume, open, move, discard, finish, repair or replace an item?
	Digital records become unreliable when physical state changes without corresponding digital updates.
	RQ — Action: What does the user actually need at a lifecycle event: consume, freeze, inspect, discard, replace, return medicine, repair, or file a warranty claim?
	“Expiry notification” is information; user value usually requires an action.
	RQ — Commonality: Which behaviors repeat across food, cosmetics, medicine and warranties?
	Defines what belongs in a shared platform core.
	RQ — Difference: Which lifecycle semantics cannot safely be generalized?
	Defines domain-specific policy modules.
	RQ — Risk/legal: Which recommendations depend on health regulation, labeling rules, disposal rules or consumer law?
	Especially important for medicine, cosmetics and warranties.
	RQ — Household coordination: Are multiple household members purchasing, opening, consuming or disposing of the same products?
	Determines whether lifecycle state needs shared ownership and history.
	RQ — Value versus burden: Does maintaining the tracker require more effort than the problem is worth to the user?
	The food-app field evidence identifies this as a major adoption risk. [5]
	RQ — Outcome: Are we measuring notification clicks, better awareness, correct lifecycle decisions, actual waste reduction, or avoided missed claims?
	These are different levels of outcome and should not be conflated.
	RQ — Product strategy: Does adding multiple domains increase retained value, or only increase cognitive and regulatory complexity?
	This is the actual cross-domain MVP decision.
	Evidence grading
I used four grades rather than treating every Google result as equivalent.
A — backbone evidence: peer-reviewed primary research with reasonably transparent sampling/method, high direct relevance and/or strong real-life observation.
B — supporting evidence: credible academic/university/large funded field work, but with small samples, incomplete peer-reviewed reporting, limited transferability, or only adjacent relevance.
C — hypothesis-generation evidence: public UX portfolios, design case studies or smaller studies whose methods are insufficiently transparent for strong claims.
D — exclude from substantive claims: promotional material, unclear methodology, secondary summaries presented as primary research, or evidence too distant from the problem.
The grade evaluates fitness for this product question, not whether the underlying research is “good science” in general. For example, a rigorous appliance-repair paper can still receive only B for a warranty-expiry-app requirement because its construct is repair behavior, not forgotten warranty deadlines.

	What is actually established
The strongest repeated food finding is not merely “people forget dates.” It is that household food management consists of planning, purchasing, storage, awareness, use and leftover management, and software that asks users to continuously reproduce that changing physical system can become another chore. Teng and Graham-Rowe establish the behavioral-management side; TotalCtrl and CozZo show the same problem emerging during actual digital interventions. [14]

The cosmetics evidence demonstrates that this is not a food-only problem, but the mechanism changes. Wang et al.'s 33 interviews directly studied why users ignore cosmetics expiry guidance and found that behavior reflects competing risk judgments rather than a simple knowledge deficit. [2] In European cosmetic labeling, an additional complication is Period After Opening (PAO): the meaningful lifecycle can depend on the event “opened” rather than solely on purchase or an absolute calendar expiry; EU guidance represents PAO with an open-jar symbol and a time period. [15]
Medicine evidence is even stronger for the existence of household-state problems. Kelly et al. used 166 semi-structured interviews with experienced Australian medicine users and found 2,301 medicines, 1,424 of which were not in everyday use; inappropriate retention, storage and disposal were sufficiently material for the authors to frame them as public-health concerns. [11] The Finnish 5,004-respondent study provides broader prevalence-oriented evidence and explicitly studies expired/unnecessary household medicines and associated factors. [16] Medicine is therefore a highly real lifecycle-management problem—but that does not make it a low-risk app vertical. FDA currently maintains dedicated guidance for disposing of unused or expired medication, demonstrating that lifecycle actions here are health-related and must be sourced from authoritative jurisdiction-specific guidance rather than generated as generic app advice. [17]
Warranty is different. The research base supports a broader repair/ownership-lifecycle problem, but I did not find an equivalent peer-reviewed interview study whose central finding is “households repeatedly lose money because they forget warranty expiry dates.” Laitala et al. is strong research—1,196 Norwegian consumers plus 15 repair-industry interviews—but it studies repair drivers and barriers rather than a warranty-tracking behavior. [4] This is a crucial evidence gap, not something that should be concealed by combining “warranty” with the better-established expiry domains.

What can genuinely be shared
Across the strongest evidence, four common mechanisms are credible.
Lifecycle state becomes cognitively invisible. Food disappears behind other items; unused medicine remains in household storage; cosmetics can continue being used without active awareness of opening/expiry state. [21]
The user has incomplete state information. Food users may not know remaining perishability; cosmetics need an opening event to operationalize PAO; medicines can become “unwanted” before the printed date; warranty eligibility does not even exist until terms, proof and failure state are combined. [22]
The lifecycle event is valuable only if it leads to an action. In food the action may be eat/freeze/cook; in cosmetics it may be continue/replace/discard; in medicine it may be follow treatment or use an authorized disposal path; in warranty it is claim/repair/replace. [23]
Maintaining state has a cost. This is directly strong in food-app studies and should be treated as a hypothesis to validate—not automatically assumed—when extending to the other domains. TotalCtrl and CozZo both show that software can create enough data-maintenance work to undermine its own value. [24]
Thus the common product should not be:
“Everything has an expiry date.”
It should be:
“Things you own pass through meaningful lifecycle events; the system helps capture enough state to surface what needs attention and the domain-appropriate action.”
That is a considerably more defensible foundation for a cross-domain product.

Scope
EXPIRY THINGS — SYSTEM SCOPE AND DEFINITION
1. System definition
Expiry Things is a domain-aware household lifecycle-management system. It maintains a digital representation of products owned or managed by a household, records lifecycle events, surfaces items that require attention, supports the next user action, and reconciles the digital record with the physical state.
The system is not defined as “an app that sends expiry notifications.” Notifications are only one capability in the larger loop: capture → understand → prioritise → act → reconcile.
The shared product hypothesis is valid only at the lifecycle-management layer. Food, cosmetics, medicine and warranty do not share one universal meaning of expiry, one clock, one status model or one safe action.


2. Scope decision and product strategy
The system uses a shared lifecycle core with domain-specific policy modules. This preserves common interaction patterns without erasing domain-specific meaning, safety rules or user actions.
* Food is the evidence-supported MVP domain because the current research has the strongest combination of household interviews, real-world app evaluations and field deployments.
* Cosmetics is a candidate second vertical, with a separate first-open / Period After Opening policy rather than a food-style expiry rule.
* Medicine is deferred until authoritative safety, disposal and content governance are available; it must not be treated as a generic discard-after-expiry flow.
* Warranty and technology-device tracking remain exploratory until direct research validates a recurring household need for this product.
* Generic consumables, documents and every other type of expiration are not automatically included in the MVP.


3. Shared lifecycle core
* Ownership and household context: who owns, adds, uses or manages an item.
* Item identity: the digital record needed to recognise and retrieve the item.
* Lifecycle events: purchase, opening, use, movement, storage change, repair, replacement or disposal when relevant.
* Attention state: what requires attention now, soon or later, without assuming that every domain uses the same status labels.
* User action: the concrete next step available to the user.
* Reconciliation: a low-friction way to update the digital record after the physical state changes.
* Feedback and preferences: user-controlled timing, frequency and correction signals that may improve prioritisation.
The shared core must not own domain-specific truth. Each domain policy defines its lifecycle clock, date meaning, evidence/provenance, state transitions, allowed actions and safety constraints.


4. Food MVP definition
For the MVP, Expiry Things manages household food and other food-related consumables with a printed, confirmed or estimated usable-until / best-before signal.
* Capture a minimum actionable record with a manual fallback.
* Preserve whether lifecycle information is printed, user-confirmed or estimated.
* Surface Needs attention, Soon and Later using food-specific prioritisation rules.
* Support validated actions such as use, cook, freeze, keep, share, discard or edit.
* Allow one-tap or low-friction reconciliation when physical food state changes.
* Group reminders by useful action to reduce notification fatigue.
* Measure whether the system creates retained value without making inventory maintenance too costly.


5. System boundary
Inside the Expiry Things boundary:
* Digital item records and lifecycle events.
* Domain policy modules and status evaluation.
* Attention prioritisation and user-controlled reminders.
* Action flows and physical-to-digital reconciliation.
* Persistent records, history and feedback needed for the product.
Outside the Expiry Things boundary:
* The physical product, packaging condition, temperature, freshness, spoilage or sensory quality.
* The user’s final decision to eat, use, keep, share, repair, replace or discard.
* Device operating-system services, system clock and third-party notification infrastructure.
* External barcode/catalogue/OCR services; these may assist capture but are not the system’s core responsibility.
* Medical, pharmaceutical, food-safety or legal authority. The system must not make unsupported safety guarantees.


6. High-level system flow
Physical product / source signal → Capture minimum record → Apply domain policy → Evaluate lifecycle state → Prioritise attention → Present an actionable next step → Reconcile physical and digital state → Learn only from sufficiently trustworthy data.
A failed barcode, OCR result or external catalogue lookup must fall back to a small manual path. The core system must remain usable without automation.


7. Business-requirement mapping
* BR-01 — Useful visibility: help users maintain awareness of items requiring attention.
* BR-02 — Low-friction capture: minimise the recurring work needed to create an actionable record.
* BR-03 — Actionable prioritisation: surface the item and moment where user action is useful.
* BR-04 — Low-friction reconciliation: reflect physical-to-digital changes with minimal interaction.
* BR-05 — Action/use support: connect an attention state to a concrete, domain-appropriate next step.
* BR-06 — Honest lifecycle uncertainty: distinguish source, certainty, estimate and domain meaning.
* BR-07 — Notification control: preserve user control over timing, grouping, frequency and disablement.
* BR-08 — Domain separation and household coordination: keep policy modules separate and add shared state only when validated.


8. Solution do / don’t
Do:
* Define the product around lifecycle visibility and action, not reminders alone.
* Use one shared interaction core with explicit domain policy boundaries.
* Keep manual fallback and correction cheaper than abandoning the record.
* Measure mechanism quality first: capture time, stale-record rate, relevant-action rate and disable/ignore rate.
* Treat Food as the current MVP and validate extensions separately.
Don’t:
* Do not use one universal Product → Expiry Date → Warning → Expired model for every domain.
* Do not claim that a date alone proves physical freshness, safety or usability.
* Do not make barcode/OCR, AI/GPT or adaptive reminders prerequisites for core value.
* Do not carry the Food MVP’s actions or safety assumptions into Medicine, Cosmetics or Warranty.
* Do not treat 40% waste reduction, 100% warranty capture or 80% response as validated user requirements without local measurement.


9. Definition of scope completion
* The shared lifecycle core and each domain policy have separate responsibilities.
* Food MVP has a defined object, lifecycle signal, attention model, action set and reconciliation path.
* Cosmetics, Medicine and Warranty are represented as explicitly deferred policy modules, not silently mixed into Food logic.
* Every requirement can be traced to a user need, evidence mechanism, candidate capability and validation metric.
* Local validation is still required before treating cross-domain behaviour, persona clusters, notification tolerance or outcome KPIs as confirmed.

Định nghĩa hệ thống
ĐỊNH NGHĨA HỆ THỐNG EXPIRY THINGS
1. System identity – Hệ thống là gì?
Expiry Things là một hệ thống phần mềm biến các sản phẩm mà người dùng đang sở hữu hoặc quản lý thành những digital representations có thể được theo dõi trong suốt vòng đời. Hệ thống kết nối user, product record, lifecycle information, time, state evaluation, attention và user action thành một whole có mục đích.
System definition: Expiry Things is a software system that represents managed products digitally, records lifecycle information and events, evaluates their current state, exposes relevant attention and supports user actions that update the record.
Định nghĩa này mô tả bản chất và cách hệ thống hoạt động; nó không quyết định domain nào được triển khai ở MVP. Các quyết định Food, Cosmetics, Medicine, Warranty và phase triển khai thuộc tab Scope.


2. System purpose – Mục đích hệ thống
* Giảm khoảng cách giữa physical reality và digital record.
* Giảm việc user phải ghi nhớ hoặc kiểm tra thủ công toàn bộ sản phẩm.
* Chuyển lifecycle information thành attention và action có thể sử dụng.
* Cho phép user cập nhật record sau mỗi thay đổi quan trọng trong physical lifecycle.
* Giữ dữ liệu đủ rõ về nguồn, độ chắc chắn và thời điểm cập nhật để user có thể tin và sửa.


3. System problem – Vấn đề mà hệ thống giải quyết
Trong đời sống, sản phẩm được mua, mở, di chuyển, sử dụng, chia sẻ, sửa chữa hoặc loại bỏ ở những thời điểm khác nhau. Thông tin về những thay đổi này thường phân tán giữa physical label, trí nhớ, không gian lưu trữ và nhiều thành viên trong household.
Nếu không có một representation tập trung, user không biết record nào còn đúng, trạng thái nào cần chú ý hoặc hành động nào nên thực hiện. Nếu việc cập nhật record quá tốn công, user sẽ bỏ qua cập nhật và hệ thống lại trở nên stale.
System-level problem: hệ thống cần vừa tạo ra visibility, vừa duy trì state accuracy, nhưng không được làm chi phí quản lý cao hơn giá trị mà nó cung cấp.


4. System boundary – Ranh giới trách nhiệm
4.1. Bên trong boundary – Expiry Things sở hữu:
* Digital product records và các thuộc tính cần thiết để nhận diện/truy xuất.
* Lifecycle information, lifecycle events và lịch sử thay đổi.
* State evaluation và attention representation.
* Action flow để user xác nhận hoặc thay đổi record.
* Notification/preference orchestration khi có capability tương ứng.
* Persistence, retrieval, correction và feedback của digital state.
4.2. Bên ngoài boundary – Expiry Things không sở hữu:
* Physical product, chất lượng vật lý, nhiệt độ, freshness, spoilage hoặc sensory condition.
* Ý chí và quyết định cuối cùng của user.
* System clock, operating system và third-party platform services.
* Độ chính xác tuyệt đối của barcode, OCR, catalogue hoặc external data provider.
* Các kết luận chuyên môn về safety, medical, legal hoặc regulatory nếu không có authority/policy tương ứng.
Boundary principle: Expiry Things quản lý digital representation và lifecycle coordination; hệ thống không được giả vờ sở hữu physical truth chỉ vì có một date hoặc một status label.


5. Core system entities – Các thực thể cốt lõi
* User: actor tạo, đọc, xác nhận, chỉnh sửa và giải quyết trạng thái.
* Household context: phạm vi sở hữu, thành viên, location và ownership khi cần.
* Product record: digital representation của sản phẩm hoặc một item/batch được quản lý.
* Lifecycle signal: thông tin giúp xác định vòng đời, có thể đến từ label, user, event hoặc external source.
* Lifecycle event: một thay đổi có ý nghĩa như create, open, use, move, share, repair, replace hoặc resolve.
* Lifecycle state: state hiện tại được hệ thống biểu diễn từ record, event, time và policy.
* Attention item: representation cho biết sản phẩm hoặc state nào cần user chú ý.
* Action: thao tác làm thay đổi physical state hoặc digital record.
* Policy: rule giải thích lifecycle signal và state transition cho một loại sản phẩm.
* Feedback: correction, preference và outcome signal do user cung cấp.


6. Relationships và data flow – Quan hệ và luồng dữ liệu
Luồng hệ thống cấp cao:
User / source signal → Product record → Lifecycle event + time → Policy evaluation → Lifecycle state → Attention → User action → Updated lifecycle event → Reconciled product record.
* User tạo hoặc cập nhật Product record.
* Product record nhận Lifecycle signal và các Lifecycle event.
* Time và Policy được dùng để đánh giá Lifecycle state.
* Lifecycle state được chuyển thành Attention item.
* Attention item cung cấp một Action có thể thực hiện.
* Action tạo event hoặc update mới.
* Update mới quay lại Product record để giảm physical–digital drift.
Điểm quan trọng: hệ thống không kết thúc ở bước gửi cảnh báo. Output có giá trị phải tạo ra một action hoặc một quyết định rõ ràng, sau đó state phải được cập nhật lại.


7. Lifecycle model – Mô hình vòng đời trừu tượng
Hệ thống dùng một lifecycle abstraction chung, nhưng không áp đặt cùng một semantic state cho mọi domain. Lifecycle abstraction gồm các phase sau:
* Captured: sản phẩm đã được đưa vào digital record.
* Known/Interpreted: hệ thống đã xác định thông tin lifecycle đang có và mức độ chắc chắn.
* Active: sản phẩm đang được user quản lý.
* Attention required: có lý do khiến sản phẩm cần được xem xét hoặc hành động.
* Actioned/Resolved: user đã thực hiện một action hoặc xác nhận trạng thái mới.
* Archived/Removed: record không còn active nhưng lịch sử có thể vẫn cần giữ.
Các tên như Fresh, Warning, Expired, Opened, PAO, Warranty hoặc những state cụ thể khác chỉ là domain policy implementation. Chúng không phải là định nghĩa universal của core system.


8. State ownership và feedback loop
System là state owner của digital record; user là authority của physical action. Hệ thống không được tự nhận rằng record đã thay đổi vật lý nếu không có event, input hoặc policy evidence phù hợp.
Feedback loop chuẩn:
* System surfaces một state hoặc attention item.
* User quan sát và thực hiện action ngoài đời hoặc trong ứng dụng.
* User xác nhận action hoặc correction.
* System ghi Lifecycle event và cập nhật Product record.
* System tính lại state và attention.
* Feedback chỉ được dùng để cải thiện prioritisation sau khi state đủ đáng tin.
Nếu user không cập nhật record, hệ thống cần thể hiện uncertainty hoặc stale risk thay vì im lặng coi record là chính xác.


9. System inputs và outputs
Inputs:
* Thông tin user nhập từ physical product hoặc source signal.
* Lifecycle event do user xác nhận.
* Time/system clock.
* Policy và rule của loại sản phẩm.
* Optional external assistance như barcode, OCR, catalogue hoặc AI.
* Feedback và notification preference.
Outputs:
* Current digital product record.
* Lifecycle state và state confidence/provenance.
* Attention list hoặc attention grouping.
* Action prompt, reminder hoặc next-step suggestion.
* Updated history sau khi user xử lý.
* Feedback signal cho các lần prioritisation tiếp theo.


10. Functional responsibility – System phải làm gì
* Tạo, đọc, sửa và lưu Product record.
* Gắn Lifecycle signal và Lifecycle event vào đúng record.
* Đánh giá state từ record, event, time và policy.
* Tạo Attention item khi state hoặc context yêu cầu.
* Đưa ra Action phù hợp với thông tin hệ thống đang có.
* Nhận correction và cập nhật lại digital state.
* Giữ manual path khi automation hoặc external provider không hoạt động.
* Duy trì history, provenance và feedback cần thiết.
Đây là responsibility của system ở cấp functional abstraction; feature cụ thể, domain cụ thể và thứ tự triển khai thuộc tab Scope và các requirement/implementation documents.


11. System quality principles – Nguyên tắc chất lượng
* Trustworthy: phân biệt confirmed, estimated, unknown và stale information.
* Low-friction: cập nhật thường ngày không được trở thành một công việc nặng hơn giá trị nhận được.
* Actionable: attention phải gắn với một next step hoặc nói rõ khi chưa biết action.
* Recoverable: lỗi barcode/OCR/external service không được làm mất manual path.
* Consistent: cùng một event phải cập nhật state và history nhất quán.
* Explainable: user hiểu vì sao một item được đưa lên attention.
* Extensible: domain mới đi qua policy boundary, không làm biến dạng core model.
* Safe by boundary: không tạo claim về physical safety, medical, legal hoặc regulatory khi không có authority.


12. System definition summary
Expiry Things là một whole system gồm User, Product record, Lifecycle signal, Lifecycle event, Time, Policy, State, Attention, Action, Persistence và Feedback. Các thành phần này tương tác để duy trì một digital representation hữu ích của những sản phẩm mà user đang quản lý.
System purpose: biến thông tin lifecycle phân tán thành một state có thể hiểu, một attention có thể ưu tiên và một action có thể thực hiện; sau đó ghi nhận action để digital record tiếp tục phản ánh physical reality.
System boundary: Expiry Things sở hữu digital lifecycle coordination, không sở hữu physical truth.
Scope decision: domain nào được triển khai, domain nào deferred và capability nào thuộc MVP được ghi riêng trong tab Scope; không trộn scope decision vào system definition.
